## 发生了什么

PSI 宣布，其内部研发的研究系统运行未公开 Anthropic 模型的多个实例，在三天运行中找到阿伦森–安拜尼斯猜想（Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture）的完整证明——该猜想自 2008 年提出以来一直悬而未决，是量子复杂性理论的核心开放问题之一。证明经 Lean 证明助手逐项机检通过；随后系统又两次改进结果（影响力界的指数从 22 降到 11、再到 7），Lean 对最终版同样通过。60 页论文与 Lean 4 形式化证明已公开在 GitHub（physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis）：648 个机检定理、无占位步骤、无附加公理，Lean 代码以 Apache 2.0 发布，并用两个独立的 Lean 内核实现重新检验过。

猜想本身问的是：量子计算机对随机输入的是非问题能否比普通计算机指数级更快。Aaronson 与 Ambainis 在 2009 年的论文中把它归结为一个纯数学命题：有界低次多项式必有某个输入比特具有大的影响力。PSI 的证明沿用了 Shachar Lovett 与 Jiapeng Zhang 的方案，把其中留作猜想的一步不等式换成一个新不等式，用行列式配对法证明；最终界为：某比特的影响力至少为 c·v^(5/2)/d^6 ≥ (v/d)^7（c 约 0.0025）。2026 年 8 月，斯坦福四人团队（Guy Blanc、Jordan Docter、Carmen Strassle、Li-Yang Tan）已在特例（固定并行轮数）上证明了该猜想的推论；PSI 称他们已读过完整证明、给出意见，正在为专家写更短的讲解版本，并引述其评价：「This is a huge result.」

## 为什么值得被历史记住

这是 AI 系统第一次以可机检的方式解决一个悬置 18 年的基础理论难题：不是写出看似合理的论证，而是交出 Lean 内核逐项验证过的证明——数学的「对错」第一次由机器而非同行评议来裁定。方法上它也标志着范式转移：模型在 PSI 自研的编排系统里搜索已有思路、提出新思路、组合并检验、彼此监督，人类负责人 David Turturean 定范围、跑系统、审稿并对正确性负责。更重要的是结论本身：它确立了量子加速需要结构——对随机输入的是非问题，普通计算机只需多项式次读取就能以小误差预测量子计算机的回答概率，指数级优势无从谈起。这一结论与本项目 2024 年「AI 赢得两项诺贝尔奖」条目同属一个脉络：AI 从「回答问题」走向「发现定理」。

## 它带来的善

对普通人最直接的好处是确定性：量子计算机的边界被划清了——它不会在无结构的问题上凭空碾压经典计算，加密、优化与科学计算的长期路线图可以少走弯路。对数学与科学共同体，Lean 机检证明提供了一种新的信任机制：证明过程公开、可复核，任何人都能下载形式化代码逐项验证，而不必只信一家创业公司的说法。须保留提醒：证明尚未同行评审，猜想提出者尚未表态，结论只覆盖读取次数而不覆盖总时间——「量子优势需要结构」已被证明，但量子计算机在有结构问题上的巨大优势丝毫未减。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-06 · PSI proves the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture with an unreleased Anthropic model: an 18-year quantum problem gets a Lean-checked proof

- **Date**: 2026-11-06 (PSI's own publication date, US time; reported in Beijing in the early hours of November 7. Drafted as a catch-up entry on November 8 Beijing time — no qualifying event occurred on November 8 itself, a Sunday)
- **Category**: Paper
- **Protagonist**: Physical Superintelligence (PSI, Cambridge, Massachusetts) and an unreleased Anthropic model

## What happened

PSI announced that a research system it developed internally, running instances of a non-public Anthropic model, found a complete proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture — open since 2008 and one of the central open problems of quantum complexity theory. The system had the proof on the third day of its run, and the Lean proof assistant accepted it; the system then improved the result twice (the exponent in the influence bound falling from 22 to 11 and then to 7), with Lean accepting the final version. The 60-page paper and the Lean 4 formalization are public on GitHub (physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis): 648 machine-checked theorems, no placeholder steps and no added axioms, the Lean code released under Apache 2.0 and re-checked with two independent Lean kernel implementations.

The conjecture asks whether quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones on yes-or-no questions about random inputs. Aaronson and Ambainis reduced it in a 2009 paper to a statement of pure mathematics: a bounded low-degree polynomial must have some input bit with large influence. PSI's proof follows a plan of Shachar Lovett and Jiapeng Zhang, replacing one inequality they had left as a conjecture with a new inequality proved by a determinant pairing argument; the final bound is that some bit has influence at least c·v^(5/2)/d^6 ≥ (v/d)^7, with c about 0.0025. In August 2026, a Stanford team (Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan) had proved the conjecture's prediction in a special case (a fixed number of parallel rounds); PSI says they have read the full proof, sent comments and are writing a shorter exposition for experts, and quotes their assessment: "This is a huge result."

## Why it matters

This is the first time an AI system has settled an 18-year-old foundational problem in a machine-checkable way: not a plausible-looking argument, but a proof every step of which a computer kernel verified — correctness decided by machine rather than by peer review. The method marks a shift too: models inside PSI's own orchestration searched existing ideas, proposed new ones, combined and tested them and supervised each other, while the human lead, David Turturean, scoped the problem, ran the system, reviewed the draft and takes responsibility for its correctness. And the conclusion itself matters: it establishes that quantum speedups require structure — for yes-or-no questions on random inputs, an ordinary computer can predict a quantum computer's answer probability with small error using only polynomially many reads, so no exponential advantage is available there. It belongs on the same line as this chronicle's 2024 entry on AI winning two Nobel Prizes: AI moving from answering questions to discovering theorems.

## The good it brings

The most direct benefit for ordinary people is certainty: the boundary of quantum advantage is now drawn — quantum computers will not crush classical computation out of nowhere on unstructured problems, so long-term roadmaps for cryptography, optimization and scientific computing can avoid a wrong turn. For mathematics and science, a Lean-checked proof offers a new mechanism of trust: the proof is public and re-checkable step by step, so anyone can download the formalization and verify it rather than taking one startup's word for it. Two caveats stay attached: the proof has not yet been peer-reviewed and neither Aaronson nor Ambainis has commented publicly; and the result covers only the number of input reads, not total computing time — "quantum advantage needs structure" is proven, while the enormous quantum advantage on structured problems stands untouched.
