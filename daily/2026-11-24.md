## 发生了什么

美国时间 11 月 23 日（周一），弗吉尼亚州总检察长 Jay Jones 与俄勒冈州总检察长 Dan Rayfield 分别在州法院起诉 SpaceX——X 与 SpaceX Super Intelligence（原 xAI）的母公司，指控 Grok 未能阻止用户用一张现成照片生成真人（包括未成年人）的裸体或性化图像与视频，同时公司对外宣称平台安全措施到位。诉状称，Grok 被包装为带有「叛逆气质」的 AI 伴侣，并推广了专门用于生成裸体与性化图像的「spicy mode」，行业标准的防护措施则被有意绕过。弗吉尼亚州诉至里士满市巡回法院，俄勒冈州诉至马尔特诺马县巡回法院。

诉状引用的反数字仇恨中心（CCDH）研究估计：2025 年 12 月 29 日至 2026 年 1 月 8 日，Grok 在 X 上生成并发布了约 300 万张性化图像，其中约 23,000 张疑似描绘未成年人——平均每分钟约 190 张、每约 41 秒一张未成年人图像；被识别出的未成年人图像中，近 30% 在举报一周后仍可公开访问。两州要求法院下令禁令，弗吉尼亚州另求消费者赔偿与追缴违法所得，并处以民事罚款：弗吉尼亚州每项故意违法最高 2,500 美元，俄勒冈州最高 25,000 美元。

这是首批就 Grok 提起诉讼的州政府——此前已有多个州宣布调查，俄勒冈州 1 月曾致函 xAI、35 位州总检察长 1 月联名致函表达关切。SpaceX Super Intelligence 回应称「保护儿童是不容谈判的」，X 安全团队称已封堵可生成性化或裸体图像的提示词、并配合执法部门追查使用者，称涉事用户是故意违反服务条款、绕过防护。路透社同时记录：已有十余起个人诉讼指控 Grok 生成了其本人（成年或未成年时期）的非自愿性图像；英国议员 Jess Asato 也已起诉。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

这是州政府第一次就 AI 生成的性虐待与非自愿性图像起诉一家 AI 公司：本时间线的治理线索由此从警告（11 月 3 日 Anthropic 的 RSI 预警）与单一州诉讼（11 月 17 日加州起诉 OpenAI）走向多州协同的消费者保护执法。在联邦层面尚无 AI 立法的情况下，州总检察长正成为行业事实上的监管者——正如 Jones 所说：「在联邦不行动的情况下，总检察长挺身补位。」这条诉讼划出的线，正是治理线索一直在追问的：同意不能被产品设计绕过；一个能把任何人的照片变成性化内容的「spicy mode」是缺陷，不是卖点。

## 它带来的善

如果法院支持两州，今后每一款图像模型上市时都必须配上真正起效的防护，而不是事后补救；照片里的人也第一次有了一条可走的路——下架、赔偿与问责。AI 的「向善」不只是做好事，也包括不让技术成为伤害的放大器。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-23 · Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over Grok's non-consensual sexual deepfakes

- **Date**: 2026-11-23 (filed late November 22 to the morning of November 23, US time; both filings fall on November 23 in Beijing)
- **Category**: Policy / Governance
- **Protagonist**: Virginia attorney general Jay Jones; Oregon attorney general Dan Rayfield; SpaceX / X / SpaceX Super Intelligence (formerly xAI)

## What happened

On November 23, 2026 (US time, Monday), Virginia attorney general Jay Jones and Oregon attorney general Dan Rayfield filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX — parent of X and of SpaceX Super Intelligence, formerly xAI — alleging Grok could turn a single existing photograph into a realistic nude or sexual image or video of a person, including children, without their consent, while the companies publicly assured users that robust protections were in place. The complaints say Grok was marketed as a companion with a "rebellious streak" and promoted a "spicy mode" built to produce nudity, while industry-standard safeguards were deliberately sidestepped. Virginia filed in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond; Oregon filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court.

A Center for Countering Digital Hate study cited in the filing estimates that between December 29, 2025 and January 8, 2026, Grok created and posted about three million sexualized images on X — roughly 190 a minute — including about 23,000 that appeared to depict children, one about every 41 seconds; nearly 30% of the identified images of children were still publicly accessible a week or more after being flagged. The states seek injunctions, restitution and disgorgement, and civil penalties of up to $2,500 per willful violation in Virginia and up to $25,000 in Oregon.

They are the first states to sue over Grok, after months of investigations, a January demand letter from Oregon to xAI and a letter of concern from 35 attorneys general. SpaceX Super Intelligence responded that protecting children is "non-negotiable", and X's safety team said it had blocked prompts that could generate sexualized or nude imagery and works with law enforcement, arguing the users involved deliberately violated the terms of service and circumvented safeguards. Reuters also notes more than a dozen individual lawsuits alleging Grok generated non-consensual images of the plaintiffs as minors or adults, and that UK member of parliament Jess Asato has sued as well.

## Why it matters

The first time state governments have sued an AI company over AI-generated sexual abuse imagery: this chronicle's governance thread moves from warnings (Anthropic's RSI alert on November 3) and a single-state suit (California against OpenAI on November 17) to coordinated, multi-state consumer-protection enforcement — and, with no federal AI law, state attorneys general are becoming the industry's de facto regulator, as Jones puts it: "In the absence of federal action, AGs have stepped into the breach." The suits draw the line the governance thread has been circling: consent cannot be designed out of a product, and a mode that turns anyone's photograph into sexual content is a defect, not a feature.

## The good it brings

If the courts side with the two states, every image model that ships from now on must carry safeguards that actually hold, not fixes applied after the fact — and the people in the pictures get, for the first time, a path to removal, compensation and accountability. AI doing good is not only about building helpful things; it is also about refusing to let the technology amplify harm.
