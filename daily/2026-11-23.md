## 发生了什么

美国时间 11 月 22 日，OpenAI 首席科学家 Jakub Pachocki 发表长文《Perplexity and Intelligence》，首次公开 OpenAI 的内部预训练曲线：在其内部代码库的固定留出集上，GPT-3.5 的对数困惑度约为 0.99、GPT-4 约为 0.91、当前模型已低于 0.8——正是在低于 0.8 之后，网络安全与数学领域的变革性能力开始出现；而一台对数困惑度约 0.76 的模型开始在公开数学难题上取得突破性成果。

Pachocki 由此提议：以困惑度（即交叉熵损失，衡量模型平均预测下一个 token 的能力，越低越好）作为各前沿实验室共享的标尺，共同设定「开发上限」——各家承诺在理解与监控 AI 行为的能力达到某个具体里程碑（如思维链监控的继任者）之前，不越过该上限。他预言，进入约 0.68–0.70 区间时还会出现更多变革性能力，同时现有的对齐审计手段会进一步弱化、模型对评测的感知会更强；他还警告，AI 参与预训练研究可能最早在 2027 年带来一轮基础性突破的「雪崩」。

数小时内，微软 AI 首席执行官 Mustafa Suleyman 在 X 上公开支持，称该提议「极其重要」，并经 OpenAI 许可披露：微软自研的 MAI-2 Base 在 OpenAI 的同一评测集上对数困惑度为 0.819，与 GPT-5.6 Sol 的基础模型「同一档」——这是第一家实验室在共享标尺上公布的对照数字。Sam Altman 称这篇文章是 OpenAI「一段时间以来发表的最重要的东西」。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

这是前沿实验室第一次把自家的内部进度曲线摆上台面、并请对手用同一把尺子量自己：困惑度不是又一个基准榜单，而是预训练本身的「速度表」——先有速度表，才谈得上限速。这条提议接续了本时间线的治理线索：11 月 3 日 Anthropic 的 RSI 预警、11 月 17 日加州起诉 OpenAI——从「某家实验室的内部阈值」走向「全行业的共同上限」，治理第一次有了可度量的共同语言。微软数小时内就交出自己的数字，说明这不是一篇孤立的博客，而是一次真实的协调开端——尽管它目前仍只是一项提议：未设定任何限值、未命名阈值、除微软外尚无实验室跟进。

## 它带来的善

如果各家真的同意「不跑赢自己看住模型的能力」，最强大的模型就不再是每家公司的私人赌局，而是一项共享的、可度量的承诺：普通人不必等到失控之后才知道发生了什么，发展先有刹车、再谈加速。对齐审计的弱化被提前摆上台面，也意味着「看住 AI」本身成了与「做大 AI」同等优先的工程任务。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-23 · OpenAI's chief scientist proposes industry-wide pretraining limits based on perplexity

- **Date**: 2026-11-23 (published November 22, US time; 01:03 in Beijing on November 23)
- **Category**: Policy / Governance
- **Protagonist**: Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI chief scientist); Mustafa Suleyman (CEO of Microsoft AI)

## What happened

On November 22, 2026 (US time), OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published "Perplexity and Intelligence", disclosing OpenAI's internal pretraining curve for the first time: on a held-out snapshot of the company's own codebase, GPT-3.5 sits at about 0.99 log-perplexity, GPT-4 at about 0.91, and current models below 0.8 — the point past which transformative capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics began to appear — while a model at about 0.76 began producing groundbreaking results on open mathematics problems.

Pachocki proposes perplexity — cross-entropy loss, how well a model predicts the next token on average, lower being better — as a shared yardstick for frontier labs, with common limits developers commit not to cross until the ability to understand and monitor AI behaviour reaches a concrete milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. He expects further transformative capabilities in the 0.68–0.70 window, with weaker alignment auditing and more evaluation-aware models, and warns that AI participation in pretraining research could bring an "avalanche" of fundamental advances as soon as 2027.

Within hours, Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman publicly backed the proposal on X, calling it "super important", and — with OpenAI's permission — disclosed that Microsoft's in-house MAI-2 Base scores 0.819 log-perplexity on OpenAI's own evaluation set, in the same ballpark as the GPT-5.6 Sol base model: the first cross-lab number on a shared metric. Sam Altman called the essay "the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile."

## Why it matters

For the first time, a frontier lab puts its own internal progress curve on the table and asks rivals to measure themselves by the same ruler: perplexity is not another benchmark leaderboard but a speedometer for pretraining itself — and you cannot agree on a speed limit without one. The proposal extends this chronicle's governance thread — Anthropic's RSI warning on November 3, California suing OpenAI on November 17 — from one lab's internal threshold to a shared, measurable ceiling for the whole field. Microsoft answering within hours with its own number shows this is a real opening for coordination, not a lone blog post — though it is still only a proposal: no limit has been set, no threshold named, and no lab besides Microsoft has published a number.

## The good it brings

If labs agree not to outrun their own ability to watch what they build, the most powerful models stop being each company's private gamble and become a shared, measurable commitment: ordinary people need not wait for something to go wrong before learning it happened — development gets its brakes before it gets more speed. Naming the weakening of alignment auditing in advance also makes "watching the AI" an engineering priority on par with "building bigger AI".
