## 发生了什么

Anthropic 在向美国证券交易委员会（SEC）提交的修订版 S-1/A 招股文件（Risk Factors 风险因素章节）中新增表述：公司用 AI 加速自身研发，可能导致模型能力进步快于安全、对齐与安保措施的跟进；由于相关证据多为滞后指标，公司「过去未能及时识别、未来也可能未能及时识别」研发速度的显著加速。文件原话：公司对未来模型的内部估计与预期（而非当前模型）显示，「我们可能很快（may imminently）达到我们《负责任扩展政策》（Responsible Scaling Policy）所定义的『可归因于 AI 的显著加速进展』」。同时文件明确：目前没有任何一款模型被认定已超过该阈值，相关数据仍在评估中。

文件还给出配套承诺：公司预计，在没有有意义的人类监督与理解的情况下，无法安全地让 Claude 完全自主地设计和构建下一代前沿模型；因此正考虑更新《负责任扩展政策》，以确保前沿模型开发始终处于人类控制之下。文件同时坦言，过去的预防措施包括推迟模型发布、限制初始可用性，未来类似措施可能更频繁、成本更高。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

这是第一家前沿实验室在具有法律效力的证券文件中，把「递归自我改进」（recursive self-improvement，RSI，即 AI 好到能训练自己、进而急剧加速自身能力）写进实质风险披露，并公开承认：用于探测这一拐点的手段本身并不可靠——加速可能在事后才被发现。RSI 此前多是辩论概念，这一次它进入了招股文件、投资者定价与监管视野：AI 治理从自愿政策声明，变成要对投资者负责的正式披露。同时这也是一次罕见的自我约束表态：一家正在冲刺上市的公司，在招股书里主动写下「人类必须留在研发回路里」，并预告可能为此放慢发布节奏、付出竞争代价。

## 它带来的善

对普通人而言，这条的意义不在某款产品，而在规则先行：当 AI 开始参与制造下一代 AI 时，率先公开承认风险、承诺人类监督并接受披露约束的公司，为整个行业立了一个可参照的标杆——能力越强，安全措施越不能掉队。需要如实保留的提醒：招股文件同时是强制披露与面向投资者的文件（TechCrunch 亦指出这一点），「接近阈值」的表述有自我宣传的一面；且文件说的是「可能很快达到内部定义」，不是「已经实现自我改进的 AI」，两者不可混淆。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-03 · Anthropic warns in its IPO filing that the recursive self-improvement (RSI) threshold may soon be met

- **Date**: 2026-11-03 (Beijing reporting date; the filing was submitted in the US on 2026-11-02, see note above)
- **Category**: Policy / governance
- **Protagonist**: Anthropic (maker of Claude, in IPO registration)

## What happened

In an amended S-1/A registration statement filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (Risk Factors section), Anthropic added language warning that its increasing use of AI to accelerate its own research and development may cause model capabilities to advance faster than its safety, alignment and security measures can follow. Much of the evidence for such acceleration comes from lagging indicators, the filing says, so the company "has in the past not identified, and may in the future not identify, a significant acceleration in our rate of development until after it has occurred." Its internal estimates and expectations for future models, rather than current ones, "indicate that we may imminently meet our Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of 'dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI.'" At the same time the filing states plainly: no current model has been designated as exceeding that threshold, and the underlying data is still being assessed.

The filing pairs the warning with a commitment: Anthropic does not anticipate being able to safely use Claude to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision or understanding, and it is considering updates to its Responsible Scaling Policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development. It also notes that past precautionary measures have included delaying model releases and limiting initial availability, and that such measures may become more frequent and more costly.

## Why it matters

This is the first time a frontier lab has put recursive self-improvement (RSI — the point at which AI becomes good enough at training itself to sharply accelerate its own capability) into a legally binding securities disclosure as a material risk, while openly admitting that the detector for that inflection point is itself unreliable: an acceleration may only be recognized after it has happened. RSI moves here from debate topic to prospectus, investor pricing and regulatory view: AI governance graduates from voluntary policy statements to formal disclosure owed to investors. It is also a rare self-restraint statement: a company racing toward listing writes into its prospectus that humans must stay in the R&D loop, and previews that it may slow releases and pay a competitive price for doing so.

## The good it brings

For ordinary people, the value is not a product but rules arriving first: as AI starts helping build the next generation of AI, a company that publicly names the risk, commits to human oversight and accepts disclosure discipline sets a reference point for the whole industry — the stronger the capability, the less safety may lag behind. One caveat must stay attached: a prospectus is both mandatory disclosure and a document addressed to investors (TechCrunch notes this too), so "near the threshold" language also serves a promotional purpose; and the filing speaks of possibly soon meeting an internal definition, not of having achieved self-improving AI — the two must not be confused.
