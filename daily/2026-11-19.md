## 发生了什么

Google 于美国时间 2026 年 11 月 18 日发布 Gemini 4 Neon（北京时间 11 月 19 日见报），Gemini 4 家族的第四款模型，也是家族中「深度与效率兼顾」的一档：Carbon 负责最难的长程项目，Hydrogen 负责快速日常执行，Neon 居于两者之间，面向复杂软件工程、知识工作与科学流程。Neon 在独立榜单 Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 上以 61 分排名第 3（xhigh 思考档），仅次于更大、更慢的模型；在 Artificial Analysis 编码智能体指数上排名第 1（58.6 分），Vals Index 第 2（70.9%），Arena 新推的 Agent Arena Index 第 2（1,336 分，基于 4,200 余次真实智能体任务）。

按 Google 自家基准，Neon 在默认 high 档下 Terminal-Bench 4.1 得 65.2%、FrontierCode 2.0 得 46.2%。定价为每百万 token 输入 3 美元、缓存输入 0.15 美元（95% 折扣）、输出 15 美元，上下文窗口 100 万 token，思考分 low、medium、high（默认）、xhigh 四档；无免费档。Google 称默认 high 档下 Neon 每任务成本比 Carbon 低 32%——Artificial Analysis 在 xhigh 档的独立测算则是智能小幅领先 Carbon、每任务成本约低 17%。

网络安全是随模型一同发布的另一半：Gemini 4 Neon for cyber（网络安全专用变体）在 SEC-bench Pro 漏洞发现上达 90.5%（同类最佳）、WebExploitBench v1.2 上达 51.26%（同类最佳），成本约为竞品大模型的一半，将在未来几天内向 Google Fairwind Program 的受信防御者开放。Neon 同日上线 Gemini API（付费版 Google AI Studio）、Gemini for business、Antigravity 与 Gemini 应用（Pro 与 Ultra 订阅用户，在应用内占据「Deeper analysis」深度分析档位，取代 Argon）；GitHub Copilot、Cursor、Vercel AI Gateway 同日接入。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

Gemini 4 家族至此补齐：七周内四款模型，各司其职——Argon 深度思考、Carbon 最难长程、Neon 兼顾深度与效率、Hydrogen 快速日常。中间档才是大多数人真正会遇到的那一款：Gemini 应用里复杂工作的默认「深度分析」档，以及独立榜单上排名第一的编码智能体模型。前沿不再是一支旗舰，而是一条产品线；竞争从「谁的模型最强」转向「每个任务有没有一款价格合适的模型」。而网络安全变体随模型一同发布、而非事后补上，延续了 Carbon 以来的模式：找漏洞、打补丁的能力成为发布标配——这条时间线里 11 月 6 日 GPT-6 Cyber 与 11 月 17 日加州起诉所记录的攻防两面，在这里合流为「防御能力随模型发货」。

## 它带来的善

直接的善是可及性与价格：前沿级编码与知识工作能力以低于 Carbon 的每任务成本进入日常工作流，开发者与知识工作者用更少的钱完成更复杂的跨文件调试、长智能体会话与 dense 文档分析；独立榜单第一的编码智能体能力意味着普通开发者也能调用到顶级的结对编程助手。网络安全变体把 90.5% 的漏洞发现率与 70.7% 的补丁修复率（CWE-bench v2）交给防御者——漏洞在被攻击者利用之前先被找到、修好。保留提醒：全部基准数字均为 Google 自报（独立榜单的排名除外），无免费档意味着最便宜的前沿仍需付费；模型卡亦提示 Neon 可能编造信息，知识截止为 2026 年 3 月。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-19 · Google releases Gemini 4 Neon: the Gemini 4 family gains its fourth tier, and the frontier becomes a product line

- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: Release
- **Protagonist**: Google DeepMind

## What happened

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, 2026 (US time; reported in Beijing on November 19), the fourth model of the Gemini 4 family and the tier that balances depth and efficiency: Carbon takes the hardest long-horizon projects, Hydrogen the fast everyday work, and Neon sits between them for complex software engineering, knowledge work and science workflows. On the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scores 61 and ranks #3 overall at its xhigh thinking level, just behind larger, slower models; it ranks #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index (58.6), #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%) and #2 on Arena's new Agent Arena Index (1,336 across 4,200+ real-world agent sessions).

On Google's own benchmarks, Neon scores 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at its default high setting. It is priced at $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached, a 95% discount) and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1-million-token context window and four thinking levels (low, medium, high by default, xhigh); there is no free tier. Google says that at the default high level Neon pairs a high Intelligence Index score with a 32% lower cost per task than Carbon — Artificial Analysis, measuring at xhigh, frames the same comparison as a small intelligence gain over Carbon at about 17% lower cost per task.

Cybersecurity is the other half of the launch: Gemini 4 Neon for cyber, a cyber variant, reaches state of the art on SEC-bench Pro (90.5% vulnerability finding) and WebExploitBench v1.2 (51.26%), at roughly half the cost of competing large models, and is rolling out over the coming days to trusted defenders in Google's Fairwind Program. Neon rolls out the same day in the Gemini API (paid Google AI Studio), Gemini for business, Antigravity and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers, where it takes the "Deeper analysis" slot in place of Argon; GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Vercel AI Gateway add it the same day.

## Why it matters

The Gemini 4 family is complete: four models in about seven weeks, one for every workload — Argon for deep thinking, Carbon for the hardest long-horizon work, Neon for depth with efficiency, Hydrogen for fast everyday execution. The middle tier is the one most people will actually meet: the "Deeper analysis" default in the Gemini app for complex work, and the top-ranked coding-agent model on an independent index. The frontier is no longer a single flagship but a product line, and the race moves from "who has the strongest model" to "who has the right model at the right price for each job." And the cyber variant ships with the model, not after it — finding and patching vulnerabilities becomes part of the launch, six days after Hydrogen and three weeks after Carbon made cyber capability a launch feature. This chronicle's November 6 GPT-6 Cyber entry and November 17 California lawsuit entry recorded the two sides of that coin — attack capability and legal consequence — and here they converge into "defense ships with the model."

## The good it brings

The direct good is access and price: frontier-level coding and knowledge-work capability reaches everyday workflows at a lower cost per task than Carbon, so developers and knowledge workers do harder multi-file debugging, long agent sessions and dense-document analysis for less money, and the top-ranked coding-agent model on an independent index becomes the pair-programming partner an ordinary developer can call on. The cyber variant hands defenders a 90.5% vulnerability-finding rate and a 70.7% patch rate (CWE-bench v2) — flaws found and fixed before attackers can use them. Caveats stay attached: all benchmark figures are Google's own, except the independent index rankings; there is no free tier, so the cheapest frontier still costs money; and the model card warns Neon can fabricate information, with a March 2026 knowledge cutoff.
