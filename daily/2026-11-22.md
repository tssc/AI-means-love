## 发生了什么

美国时间 2026 年 11 月 20 日，Anthropic 数学家 Levent Alpöge 宣布，他与 Claude 合作找到了黎曼猜想（Riemann Hypothesis，1859 年提出）及 Dirichlet L-函数广义黎曼猜想的证明，并以 Lean 4 代码的形式发布——175,187 个文件、约 1,700 万行代码，没有传统论文，只有 alpo.ge 上的一页简短说明。

北京时间 11 月 21 日 21:55（13:55 UTC），Lean 与 Mathlib 社区发表联合声明（Lean FRO 与 Mathlib Initiative 背书）：「我们完全相信 Alpöge 对黎曼猜想的证明是正确的……我们特此证明，黎曼猜想的陈述与证明都是正确的。」（"We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct."）

独立核查由 MIT 数学家 Andrew Sutherland 汇总（验证页截至 11 月 22 日 04:35 UTC）：175,187 个模块全部编译通过、零错误（Lean v4.33.1、Mathlib v4.33.1）；Main.lean 中的五条主定理仅依赖 Lean 的三条标准公理（propext、Classical.choice、Quot.sound）；独立编写的内核 nanoda、con-leche 与 lean4lean 均接受完整导出的证明（61.6 GB）；comparator 的两种独立实现（con-leche --compare、con-ron --compare）均确认所证明的定理与挑战文件中的陈述一致——挑战陈述只导入 Mathlib，数学概念（riemannZeta、DirichletCharacter 等）均为 Mathlib 自身的定义；沙箱中独立重建（lake build）逐字节复现了导出的证明。五条定理包括：黎曼猜想本身（Mathlib 定义）、同一陈述的另一种写法、所有 Dirichlet L-函数在临界线右侧无零点、临界带内的零点均在临界线上、以及本原特征标的情形（即 Dirichlet L-函数的广义黎曼猜想；Dedekind zeta 与一般自守 L-函数不在核查范围内）。

还有一道不依赖 Mathlib zeta 定义的语义交叉检验：把该证明与独立的 Robin1984 形式化结合，推出 Robin 不等式与 Lagarias 不等式——这两条关于除数和的不等式与黎曼猜想等价，且陈述中不出现 zeta 函数；其 comparator 已于 11 月 22 日 04:16 UTC 通过。Scientific American 11 月 21 日更新报道：「数学家们现在确信……证明是真实的。」

限定与分歧，如实记录：① 尚无人类可读的论文，数学界还没有理解、审阅这个论证，联合声明本身也邀请学界来解释证明中的新思想；② 克雷数学研究所未改变立场，100 万美元千禧年大奖未变；③ 两项机检仍在进行中（lake comparator 端到端运行、Lean 内核对导出的重放，均为单线程长耗时任务）；④ 质疑已经出现：11 月 21 日 Jenny Lorraine Nielsen 在 PhilArchive 发文《Invalidity of the Alpoge Proposed Proof》，称证明所用的几何谓词被定义为恒真（`anyGeometry := fun _ _ _ => True`），见证空洞；11 月 22 日 Frank Chu 在 dev.to 核实：该定义确实存在于代码中、用于 24 个文件，而真正的几何谓词（geoOf）在 12 个文件中——但他同时说明，决定性的问题（Main.lean 的依赖链是否经过该定义）尚未查清，「仍是开放问题」；「退化模型」的说法他未能证实；截至 11 月 22 日，Alpöge 与 Lean 社区尚无公开回应；Nielsen 并非该领域知名人物，论文未经同行评审，且她同日还对 Masullo 的人类证明发了类似的无效性主张。⑤ 同日还出现了竞争性的全人类证明：斯坦福博士后 Alessandro Masullo 的 940 页手稿（12 年工作），由美国数学会主席 Ravi Vakil 于 11 月 20 日发布并注明「全人类、无 AI」；但发布约 8 小时内就有人报告了其中的问题（部分由 AI 模型协助发现），Masullo 本人承认手稿「需要重大修订」、尚未达到发表状态——它不是 AI 事件，不另立条，仅作背景。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

167 年前黎曼写下那个关于素数分布的猜想时，没有人能想到它可能由一位数学家与一个 AI 合作攻克——更没有人能想到，证明本身会以 1,700 万行无人读过的代码出现，而验证只用了一天、由机器完成。这条记录值得记住的正是验证本身：多内核独立重放、陈述比对、沙箱重建、与 Robin/Lagarias 不等式的语义交叉检验——这套流程是 2026 年 7 月一次教训的直接产物：当时一份伪造的 Lean「证伪 Collatz 猜想」利用内核漏洞通过了 Lean 自身的检查，之后才有了多检查器 + comparator 的标准动作。如果证明成立，它改变数学证明的样子；如果质疑成立，它同样改变世界检验 AI 产出数学的方式。而同一个周末的对照令人难忘：一份「全人类、无 AI」的 940 页手稿几小时内就被发现问题、承认需要大修——机检不是可有可无的点缀，是这个时代的数学得以成立的条件。

## 它带来的善

一个任何人都能重新跑一遍的证明——代码公开、约 100GB 内存与 70GB 磁盘即可复核——把最高水平的数学从「少数专家的判断」变成了「任何人都能验证的事实」：学生、发展中国家的研究者、不在顶尖圈子里的数学家，都能亲自检验 167 年来最著名的猜想是否成立，而不必等待权威的点头。更重要的是，独立内核与 comparator 这套开源验证工具，从此成为所有 AI 产出数学声明的标尺：AI 说它证明了什么，不再靠口头承诺，而靠任何人都能复现的机器检验。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-21 · Lean and Mathlib communities certify the Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

- **Date**: 2026-11-21 (claim: November 20, US time; certification: November 21, 13:55 UTC / 21:55 Beijing)
- **Category**: Paper / Scientific discovery
- **Protagonist**: Levent Alpöge (Anthropic mathematician) with Claude; the Lean and Mathlib communities

## What happened

On November 20, 2026 (US time), Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced that he and Claude had found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (posed in 1859) and of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, released only as Lean 4 code — 175,187 files, about 17 million lines, with no traditional paper, only a short note at alpo.ge.

On November 21 at 13:55 UTC (21:55 in Beijing), the Lean and Mathlib communities published a joint statement, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: "We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct... We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct."

Independent verification, tracked by MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland (page updated to November 22, 04:35 UTC): all 175,187 modules compile with zero errors (Lean v4.33.1, Mathlib v4.33.1); the five headline theorems in Main.lean depend only on Lean's three standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound); independently written kernels — nanoda, con-leche and lean4lean — accept the full exported proof (61.6 GB unpacked); two independent implementations of the statement comparator (con-leche --compare and con-ron --compare) confirm the theorems proved match the challenge statements, which import only Mathlib and use Mathlib's own definitions (riemannZeta, DirichletCharacter, and the rest); and an independent sandboxed rebuild reproduces the export byte for byte. The five theorems cover the Riemann Hypothesis itself (Mathlib's definition), the same statement written out, no zeros to the right of the critical line for every Dirichlet L-function, zeros in the critical strip lying on the critical line for every Dirichlet L-function, and the same for primitive characters — the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions; Dedekind zeta functions and general automorphic L-functions are out of scope.

There is also a semantic cross-check that does not depend on Mathlib's definition of the zeta function: combining the proof with an independent formalisation of Robin's and Lagarias's criteria yields the Robin and Lagarias inequalities — statements about divisor sums, equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis, that never mention zeta — and its comparator passed at 04:16 UTC on November 22. Scientific American, updating on November 21: "Mathematicians are now certain the... proof... is real."

Limits and disputes, stated in the record: (1) there is no human-readable paper yet; the mathematical community has not understood or reviewed the argument, and the joint statement itself invites mathematicians to explain the proof's new ideas; (2) the Clay Mathematics Institute has not changed its position on the $1 million Millennium Prize; (3) two machine checks are still running (the end-to-end lake comparator run and the Lean kernel's replay of the export, both slow single-threaded jobs); (4) a challenge has been raised: on November 21 Jenny Lorraine Nielsen posted a PhilArchive paper arguing the proof's geometry predicate is defined as trivially true (`anyGeometry := fun _ _ _ => True`) and its witnesses vacuous; on November 22 Frank Chu verified in the code that the definition exists and is used in 24 files, while the real geometry predicate sits in 12 — but he states plainly that the decisive question, whether Main.lean's dependency path runs through that definition, is still open, that he could not confirm the paper's "degenerate model" wording, and that as of November 22 there is no public response from Alpöge or the Lean community; Nielsen is not a known figure in the field, the paper is not peer-reviewed, and she filed a similar invalidity claim against Masullo's human proof the same day. (5) The same weekend produced a competing all-human proof: Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo's 940-page draft (twelve years of work), posted by American Mathematical Society president Ravi Vakil on November 20 as "all human, no AI" — but problems were reported within about eight hours, and Masullo concedes it needs major revision and is not ready for publication; it is not an AI event and gets no separate entry, only this context.

## Why it matters

One hundred and sixty-seven years after Riemann wrote down his conjecture about the primes, it may have fallen to a mathematician working with an AI — and the verification is itself the story: a proof no human has yet read was machine-checked within a day, by several independent checkers, under a protocol built for exactly this case after a forged Lean "disproof" of the Collatz conjecture slipped past Lean's own kernel in July 2026. If the proof stands, it changes what a mathematical proof looks like; if the challenge lands, it still changes how the world vets AI-produced mathematics. The same weekend's contrast is hard to forget: a 940-page "all human, no AI" draft needed major revision within hours of appearing — machine checking is not an ornament on mathematics in this era, it is what makes it hold.

## The good it brings

A proof anyone can re-run — the code is public, needing roughly 100 GB of RAM and 70 GB of disk — turns the most famous conjecture in mathematics from "a verdict handed down by a few experts" into "a fact anyone can verify for themselves": students, researchers far from the top circles, anyone at all can check whether 167 years of open problem is closed, without waiting for authority to nod. And the open verification tooling — independent kernels and the comparator — now sets the bar for every AI claim of a proof: an AI says it proved something, and the answer is no longer a promise but a machine check anyone can reproduce.
