## 发生了什么

Thinking Machines Lab 于美国时间 2026 年 11 月 20 日发布 Inkling 2 Mini——一款 26.4B 参数的开放权重循环 Transformer（looped transformer）：24 层中，中间 12 层以共享权重每 token 跑三遍，24 层权重产生 48 次层应用；每 token 激活 3.4B 参数，计入循环为 5.4B 有效激活参数；上下文 100 万 token。模型大部分权重以 NVFP4 训练与部署（最后 4 层 MXFP8），总内存占用约 20GB，可在消费级笔记本上离线运行——官方研究员在航班上用一台 MacBook Pro（M5 Pro，48GB）离线运行它，几分钟内做出一个可玩的飞行模拟器。模型以 Apache 2.0 许可开放权重，上线 Hugging Face 与自家 Tinker 平台（推理与微调）。

官方口径基准（未经独立复现）：在 100B 总参数以下的开放权重模型中，智能体编码 DeepSWE v1.1 得 48.2%、SWE-bench Verified 得 79.2%，Vals Index v2.2 得 46.5%，均居同档第一；AA Intelligence Index v5.0 得 20.1 分，与 Qwen3.8-27B 并列第一。局限同样写在官方博客里：多轮对话与多约束指令上弱于 Qwen3.8-27B，知识型基准受参数规模所限，推理算力高于激活参数数所暗示的水平。

同日还发布了一个变循环研究模型 TML-d24-varloop-research（循环次数可在推理时 1–5 遍调节），专为研究循环对可监测性的影响：无思维链（no-CoT）时间跨度从 1 遍的 16 秒升至 3 遍的 37 秒，串行推理在 10 遍时仍在提升；思维链随循环变短但始终可读，智能体破坏（sabotage）测试分数未随循环增加而上升。官方结论：循环增加了无需写出思维链即可完成的推理，但在这个规模上未见可监测性有实质下降。

## 为什么值得被历史记住

循环 Transformer 从 2018 年 Universal Transformers 的论文构想，走进一款可下载、可本地运行的开放模型：不靠堆参数、靠「把中间层多跑几遍」换取更深的单步推理，前沿级智能体编码能力第一次装进 20GB、跑上普通人已有的笔记本。这是 Inkling 家族的另一条路——11 月 5 日的 Inkling 2 Small（594B）往大走，15 天后的 Mini 往小走，而「小」的那条恰恰更接近普通人：不用云端、不用交数据、不用付推理账单，学生与独立开发者也能在本地跑一个像样的编码智能体。更重要的是，实验室把循环架构引出的安全问题——藏在循环里的推理会不会逃过思维链监测——连同测量数据一起公开，而不是只给结论：这是一种可被独立检验的安全主张，也是开放权重真正的意义。

## 它带来的善

一个能在笔记本上离线跑的强编码模型，把「好用的 AI 助手」从云端账单里解放出来：学生、独立开发者与预算有限的小团队不用租 GPU、不用把代码发给远方服务器，也能在本地获得一个能写代码、会用工具的助手；对隐私敏感的工作（病历、合同、未公开的研究），数据不必出门。同时，开放权重加公开的可监测性研究，让任何人都能自己验证「循环会不会藏住推理」——安全不再只是实验室的口头承诺。

## English version (canonical)

# 2026-11-21 · Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini, a looped transformer that runs on a laptop

- **Date**: 2026-11-21 (released November 20, US time; reported in Beijing on November 21)
- **Category**: Release / Open source
- **Protagonist**: Thinking Machines Lab

## What happened

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, 2026 (US time), a 26.4B-parameter open-weights looped transformer: the middle 12 of its 24 layers run three times per token with shared weights, producing 48 layer applications from 24 layers of weights, with 3.4B active parameters per token (5.4B effective with looping) and a 1M-token context. Trained and deployed mostly in NVFP4 (the last four layers in MXFP8), the model fits in about 20GB and runs offline on a consumer laptop — the lab's researchers ran it on a MacBook Pro (M5 Pro, 48GB) during a flight, building a playable flight simulator in minutes. It is released under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face and via the Tinker platform (inference and fine-tuning).

Company-reported benchmarks, not independently reproduced: among open-weights models under 100B total parameters, it leads on agentic coding with 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified, and 46.5% on the Vals Index v2.2; on the AA Intelligence Index v5.0 it scores 20.1, tied for first with Qwen3.8-27B. The official blog states the limits too: weaker than Qwen3.8-27B on multi-turn chat and many-constraint instructions, knowledge benchmarks bounded by its parameter count, and compute per token higher than the active-parameter count suggests.

A variable-loop research model, TML-d24-varloop-research (loop count adjustable from 1 to 5 passes at inference time), was released the same day to study what looping does to monitorability: the no-chain-of-thought time horizon rises from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 seconds at three, serial reasoning keeps improving out to ten loops, the chain of thought shortens with more loops but stays legible, and agentic sabotage scores do not rise with more loops. The lab's conclusion: looping increases the reasoning a model can do without writing out its chain of thought, but at this scale it sees no meaningful reduction in the monitorability of its reasoning.

## Why it matters

The looped transformer steps out of a 2018 paper (Universal Transformers) and into a downloadable model that runs on hardware people already own: depth instead of size, more reasoning per token without more parameters, so frontier-class agentic coding fits in 20GB. Fifteen days after the 594B Inkling 2 Small went big, Mini goes small — and small is the direction that reaches ordinary people: no cloud bill, no data leaving the machine, no GPU rental, a serious coding agent on a laptop. Just as important, the lab publishes the safety question the architecture raises — whether reasoning inside loops escapes chain-of-thought monitoring — together with the measurements, not just the conclusion: a safety claim others can check for themselves, which is what open weights are for.

## The good it brings

A strong coding model that runs offline on a laptop frees a good AI assistant from the cloud bill: students, indie developers and small teams on tight budgets can have a coding, tool-using assistant locally without renting GPUs or sending their code to a distant server, and for privacy-sensitive work — medical records, contracts, unpublished research — the data never has to leave the room. And open weights plus published monitorability research let anyone verify for themselves whether looping hides reasoning — safety stops being only the lab's word for it.
